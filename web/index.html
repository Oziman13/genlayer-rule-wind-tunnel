          How the measurement works

          The contract is a single Python file. Its source, tests and a recorded run are in the repository.

          Source repository
          Contract on the explorer

          1. One question per wording

            Each validator asks its model, once for each wording, to say whether every case complies with the rule, violates it, or is left unclear by it. That makes a table: wordings down, cases across.

          2. Agreement on the table

            Each validator asks its own model; the table is accepted when the grade of the leader's table and its own grade are at most one step apart (cells may differ). A pack on which models disagree widely does not settle. The numbers below are computed from the agreed table with whole-number arithmetic.

          3. Consistency and fidelity

            Consistency is how often two wordings give the same verdict to the same case. Fidelity is how often the majority verdict matches what you intended. The grade comes from the lower of the two.

          4. A record you can point to

            The pack is stored under the hash of its text. The same pack can be measured again, up to five times, five minutes apart, so you can see how much the readings move between runs.

          What it does not tell you

          • A steady grade is about these cases and these wordings. A rule can read steadily here and still fail on a case you did not think of.
          • The model is the one the validators run. A different model, or the same model later, can read the rule differently.
          • It measures the reading, not whether your intent is right.
          • A table that sits right on a grade edge may not settle, because the validators must agree on the grade. Add cases to move it away from the edge.
          • Verdicts that do not follow the required format are never recorded: the measurement is reported as unmeasurable and nothing is stored.
          • Anyone can measure any pack. The record names who ran each measurement, not who wrote the rule.
